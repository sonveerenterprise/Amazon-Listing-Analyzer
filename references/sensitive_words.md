# Sensitive Claims Check

Sensitive words are the opposite of keywords: words that must not appear, or must be worded carefully. A word with search value **and** a sensitive meaning (warranty, disease terms) is **never** recommended as a keyword and is never counted as a "missing keyword".

Scan every field: title, Item Highlights, bullets, A+ text and images, attributes, and text inside carousel images.

## 1. India (amazon.in) — FSSAI and consumer law

Based on FSSAI Advertising & Claims Regulations 2018, Labelling & Display Regulations 2020, Organic Food Regulations 2017, and the Consumer Protection Act 2019. These are risk flags; the seller should confirm with a food-regulatory consultant.

| Claim / word | Risk | Notes and safer wording |
|---|---|---|
| Disease or treatment claims: "cures", "treats", "prevents", "for diabetes", "diabetic friendly", "lowers blood sugar", "reduces cholesterol", "heart health", "cardiovascular" | **P0** | Not allowed on general foods. Remove. Use neutral facts ("no added sugar", "keto friendly"). |
| Health-benefit claims: "boosts immunity", "improves digestion", "glowing skin", "detox", "protects cells from oxidative stress" | P1 | Need scientific substantiation / approval. Use "contains naturally occurring antioxidants", "a daily addition to your routine". |
| "Pure" | P1 | Only for single-ingredient foods with nothing added. Not for blends (e.g. honey + turmeric) or products with preservatives. |
| "Natural" | P1 | Only for single foods from a natural source with nothing added; composites may say "made from natural ingredients". |
| "Fresh", "original", "authentic", "traditional" | P2 | Need support or remove as filler. |
| "Rich in X" / "High in X" / "Source of X" | P1 | Must meet thresholds (source ≈ 15% RDA per 100 g or 7.5% per 100 ml; rich/high ≈ twice that). Check against the nutrition table. |
| "Zero sugar", "sugar free", "zero calorie", "low calorie", "low fat" | P1 | Must match the nutrition table (zero calorie ≈ ≤4 kcal/100 g or ml). |
| "Low glycemic index" | P1 | Needs GI test data. |
| "Organic" | P1 | India requires NPOP or PGS-India certification and the Jaivik Bharat logo. USDA alone isn't enough for domestic sale; lead with India Organic. |
| "Lab certified", "NABL certified" | P1 | Products aren't certified by NABL; labs are accredited. Use "tested at an NABL-accredited lab" only if true. |
| "FSSAI approved" | P1 | FSSAI licenses businesses; it doesn't approve products. Use "FSSAI Lic. No. …". |
| Comparisons that criticise other product types on health grounds | P1 | Remove health rows; keep factual rows (taste, ingredients). |
| Infant use | P1 | Honey must carry "not for infants under 1 year"; repeat it in text. |
| "Made in India" | P1 | Must match the country of origin. |

## 5. Warranty statements (all marketplaces)

Amazon policy:
1. **Title:** never mention a warranty (P0).
2. **Images:** no warranty or guarantee badges or text (P0).
3. **Bullets / A+:** no guarantee promises such as "full refund", "unconditional guarantee", "lifetime guarantee", "100% guarantee" (P0).
4. **Seller's own warranty** can't be promoted on the detail page (P1).
5. A manufacturer warranty can be mentioned only with terms, duration, region and provider name and address; otherwise don't mention it.
6. **Where to put a real warranty:** US — product edit page → Safety & Compliance → Warranty Description. India — Growth → Browse programs → Add Warranty info (Excel upload; shown as a separate warranty card).

Warranty is never a required bullet; don't penalise its absence. Build trust with verifiable facts instead: what's in the box, dimensions, materials, test data, "returns per Amazon's return policy".

## 6. Process

1. Scan all fields, including A+ and image text (A+ is checked separately; compliant bullets don't make the A+ compliant).
2. Classify each hit and give a risk level.
3. Give safer wording for every hit.
4. Remove sensitive words from keyword suggestions; note "excluded: sensitive".
